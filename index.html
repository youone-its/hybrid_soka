Part 1 · The story · 3/5 · Trick 2

👣 Trick 2: leave footprints, never step back on them

You start at the bottom of the valley where the helicopter left you. Press Step.

① Look at the spots around you and go to the best one, even if it's uphill.

② Leave a 👣 footprint where you were. You can't step on it for a few steps, so you can't slide back into the same hole.

③ Footprints melt after 5 steps. ✨ Exception: you may step on one if it's a new record.

☁️ In the real algorithm
👣 footprints = tabu list · melting = tenure · exception = aspiration. Together: Tabu Search.

Part 1 · The story · 4/5 · Trick 3

🔦 Trick 3: whenever you break a record, search the area thoroughly

You just reached a new record low ⭐. Before moving on, use your 3 tools here.
🔦
Tool 1 · flashlight
check the spots right next to you
🔭
Tool 2 · binoculars
check spots a bit further away
🪂
Tool 3 · paraglider
try a few spots far away

Found something lower? Go there and start again with tool 1. Nothing found? Try the next tool. When all 3 fail, the area is fully searched.

☁️ In the real algorithm
the 3 tools = 3 neighbourhoods · this ladder = VND (Variable Neighborhood Descent).

What does "good" mean?

One score, F(x), from four goals. Lower is better.

Pick a schedule

Weights

How the score works

Each goal is compared with HEFT: 1.00 = same as HEFT, 1.20 = 20% worse, 0.90 = 10% better.

Then each ratio is multiplied by its weight (how much we care), and the results are added up.

HEFT itself scores 0.4 + 0.2 + 0.2 + 0 = 0.80. That's the number to beat.

Formula in evaluation/objectives.py:
F = w₁·T/THEFT + w₂·C/CHEFT + w₃·E/EHEFT + w₄·SLA

Why not just try every schedule?

Because there are way too many: the mountains are huge

Our toy: 16 tasks × 5 VMs05¹⁶ schedules · ≈ 1.8 days at 1 million/sec

Stage 2 · 🌫️ The spots you can see in the fog

What is one "step"? Four kinds of moves

👉 Click a move type on the right to see an example applied to the HEFT schedule.
🎯 Critical-path move: a task on the chain that decides the finish time goes to a less busy VM.
🔥 Bottleneck move: a task leaves the busiest VM.
💰 Cheaper-VM move: a task goes to the VM that is cheapest in cost + power for it.
🔄 Swap: two tasks on different VMs trade places.

Each round, Tabu Search builds about 15 such candidates (the spots visible in the fog), scores them all, and steps to the best one that isn't tabu (no 👣). Afterwards the task may not go back to its old VM for 7 rounds.

Honest analysis

Where HEFT-TS-VND wins, where it loses, and why

✅ Strengths

⚡ Fastest search method: about 2× faster than ALNS and DHA-ALNS at the same evaluation budget.

🥇 Ties for best on alibaba_small (0.7928, same as ALNS).

📏 Keeps HEFT's finish time exactly: it never trades finish time away.

🔁 Low variance: best-move choice and memory instead of random restarts.

⚠️ Weaknesses

💸 On alibaba_medium it ends with a higher cost (1.76 vs 1.65 for ALNS), so its score is worse (0.785 vs 0.773).

🌫️ About 15 evaluations per round means only about 100 rounds within 1,500 evaluations. The "fog" is too thick.

🎯 About half of the candidates are critical-path moves, but HEFT's finish time is already near-optimal. The gains are in cost and energy.

🔧 Next improvements

Spend more of the candidate list on cheaper-VM and swap moves, or adapt the mix to the objective weights.

Incremental evaluation (re-simulate only the affected tasks) to afford more rounds.

Tune tenure and candidate-list size on all 4 scenarios.

🧩 Why the earlier DHA-ALNS lagged

100–300 tasks is too small for decomposition to pay off. Clusters share VMs and data links, so merged results clash. Workers restart their search every round. On alibaba_medium: 0.779 ± 0.002 vs 0.773 ± 0.002 for ALNS.
